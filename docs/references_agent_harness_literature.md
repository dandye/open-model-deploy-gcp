## Harness self-improvement and its regularization

### Xu et al. (2026). Harness^2: Recursive Agent Harnessing for an Open World

Ruiyao Xu, Yanfei Chen, Zhongying CuiZhu, Bhavana Dalvi Mishra, Yifei Ming, Han Yu, Rujun Han, Chen-Yu Lee, Tomas Pfister. Northwestern University & Google Cloud AI Research. arXiv:2610.14224. Code: `https://github.com/google-research/harness2`. Project page: `https://harness2.github.io`.
KB: `wiki/papers/xu_2026.md`.

$\text{Harness}^2$ extends recursive harness self-improvement to open-world task streams by separating the *execution harness* $\mathcal{H}_0$ (reset cleanly per task across an 8-component runtime surface: system prompt, guardrails, memory, tool guidance, subagents, skills, scripts, and plugins) from a persistent *continual improvement harness* $\mathcal{R}_t$ (an indexed library of adopted harness trees and contrast-supported `EXPERIENCE.md` lessons). It connects two levels of recursion while keeping model weights fixed:

1. **Task-level recursion (`Propose-Probe-Compose`)**: Given a baseline rollout $e^0$, two scoped editors propose candidate harness revisions in parallel: **`INVARIANT` (`INV`)** targets general agent workflow, tool ordering, and verification procedures, while **`DOMAIN` (`DOM`)** targets practitioner rules and domain decision contracts. Both candidates are probed under matched sandbox execution settings, and a composer selectively merges empirically supported edits from their behavioral contrasts (or returns `KEEP_BASE`). Task-level recursion scales via parallel recursion ($k$ base runs, $2k$ probes, $k$ compositions) or sequential recursion ($k$ chained Propose-Probe-Compose steps).
2. **Harnessing-level recursion (`Experience Reflector`)**: After each step, an Experience Reflector diffs candidate components against $\mathcal{H}_0$, assigns each touched component a 4-state attribution label (`helped | hurt | no observed effect | confounded`, defaulting to `confounded` when multi-component edits lack isolated rollouts), and appends contrast-supported rules (`cue`, `lesson`, `evidence`, `confidence`) to `EXPERIENCE.md` alongside $\mathcal{H}_t^\star$ in $\mathcal{R}_{t+1}$.

Numbers checked against the arXiv v1 PDF (Tables 1–3, 6–7): across WorkBuddy-Bench (100 tasks: 40 Code, 35 Web, 25 Office), JobBench (34 tasks), and Harvey LAB (100 tasks), one step ($k=1$) improves frozen `Gemini 3.5 Flash` and `GLM-5` executors across both `OpenCode` and `Codex` substrates (up to `+11.5` points on WorkBuddy-Bench `64.2 -> 75.7`, `+4.5` on JobBench `31.5 -> 36.0`, and `+11.8` percentage points on LAB `59.6 -> 71.4`), with $k=5$ adding another `+2.6` to `+6.7` points and sitting on the total-token Pareto frontier ahead of `Meta-Harness`, `ReasoningBank`, and `Harness Scaling`. Ablations at $k=1$ (WorkBuddy `77.5` / JobBench `36.0`) confirm every component of the design carries weight: removing the experience library drops `-3.1` / `-1.8`, removing `INV`/`DOM` role scoping drops `-2.9` / `-3.7`, and removing composition drops `-1.6` / `-1.9`.

Used here as the architecture for `Harness2ProposeProbeComposeProposer` in [`agent/gepa_runbook_optimizer.py`](../agent/gepa_runbook_optimizer.py) and `attribute_candidate_edits()` / `ExperienceLibrary` in [`agent/hillclimb_harness.py`](../agent/hillclimb_harness.py), adapted to multi-runbook SOC triage and proactive threat hunting with two domain-specific extensions: (1) `ComponentStratifiedBatchSampler` + `NonFrozenRoundRobinSelector` to prevent minority runbooks (`malware_triage.md`, `lateral_movement_hunt_psexec_wmi.md`) from starving in outer GEPA minibatches, and (2) categorical safety/taxonomy gating inside `_probe_score()` so `INV`/`DOM`/`COMPOSED` candidates that trigger `OVER_CONTAINMENT_FALSE_ALARM` or `RUNBOOK_PROCEDURAL_VIOLATION` are rejected during inner probing (`-1.0`). Full empirical results are in [`HARNESS2_CAMPAIGN_REPORT.md`](soc_eval_results/harness2_8h_campaign/HARNESS2_CAMPAIGN_REPORT.md).

### Xia et al. (2026). RRSI: Regularized Recursive Self-Improvement of Agent Harnesses

Peng Xia, Rujun Han, Zifeng Wang, Yanfei Chen, Yufan Zhuang, Yoonho Lee, Chengsong Huang, Han Yu, Zhongying CuiZhu, Yifei Ming, Huaxiu Yao, Burak Gokturk, Tomas Pfister, Chen-Yu Lee. Google Cloud AI Research. arXiv:2609.24972. Code: `github.com/google-research/rrsi`. Project page: `regularized-rsi.com`.
KB: `ingest_rrsi_2026_papers/wiki/papers/xia_2026.md` and `wiki/concepts/regularized_recursive_self_improvement.md`. Note the KB frontmatter spells the fifth author "Zhang"; arXiv has "Zhuang".

The paper treats harness evolution as an estimation problem and regularizes both sides of the loop. Proposal side: annealed update sparsity (an edit budget `b_t` that shrinks from `b_max` to `b_min`), evidence-aware credit assignment (the proposer conditions on a ledger of component, hypothesis, diff, score, cost, accepted), and structured exploration (stall detection inside the noise band plus reserved budget for unexercised components). Selection side: leakage screening by an LLM critic that reads the diff before evaluation, stability-aware acceptance against a noise band `delta` estimated by re-evaluating the unchanged base, Ridge-style complexity-aware acceptance (allowed cost increase `beta_0 + beta_1 * delta_S`), and Lasso-style structural pruning of components with no positive gain over a window. All four agent roles (policy, proposer, analyst, critic) are Claude Opus 4.8. Eight benchmarks: Terminal-Bench 2.1, SWE-bench Verified, Harvey LAB, JobBench, GDPval, APEX-Agents, EngDesign, Frontier-Eng.

Numbers checked against the arXiv v2 HTML: evolve-set gains of 6.0 (Terminal-Bench 2.1), 4.9 (EngDesign), 1.1 (Harvey LAB); SWE-bench Verified +1.8; three OOD agentic benchmarks +3.5 to +4.7; no held-out split regresses. Ablation: removing acceptance constraints moves evolve-set 90.5 to 91.5, OOD 43.6 to 41.0, tokens up about 50%; removing all constraints gives evolve-set 92.8, OOD 40.3, 3.80M tokens per trial against RRSI's 2.42M. Cross-backbone: harnesses evolved on Opus 4.8 raise Gemini 3.5 Flash on Terminal-Bench 2.1 from 64.6 to 78.7 and Gemini 3.1 Flash Lite from 11.2 to 14.6. Limitations the authors state: frozen weights only, finite evolve set, hyperparameters.

Used here as the reference design for guards 2 and 5 and as the source of the gap list in the crosswalk. The ablation row is the single most useful number in the paper for this project: it is our 2026-09-04 versus Campaign 5 story, measured properly.

### Ding et al. (2026). Harness-Delta Attribution: What Evolves When We Talk About Harness Evolution?

Wenxuan Ding, Q. Lu, C. Yu, S. Li, S. Jin, X. Liu, Greg Durrett. UT Austin. `https://wenwen-d.github.io/blog/harness-delta-attribution/`.
KB: `ingest_rrsi_2026_papers/wiki/papers/ding_2026.md`.

Decomposes any measured harness improvement into a genuine generalizable delta, a test-time compute delta, and an artifact or overfitting delta, and argues that much of what is reported as harness improvement is the second and third. The page's meta description was verified; the specific percentages the KB page quotes could not be re-verified against the source (the fetch returned only stylesheets), so this repository cites the three-way decomposition and not the magnitudes.

Used in the crosswalk to read our three results: the 2026-09-04 in-distribution gain (cost went down, so not compute), Campaign 5 (gain did not transfer, so artifact), and the 2026-10-02 run (nothing accepted, all deltas zero).

### Zhang and Khattab (2026). Language Model Harnesses Are Compositional Generalizers

Alex L. Zhang, Omar Khattab. `https://alexzhang13.github.io/blog/2026/harness/`.
KB: `ingest_rrsi_2026_papers/wiki/papers/zhang_alex_2026.md`.

Harness behavior generalizes, or fails to, at the level of how components compose. Our evidence for this is Campaign 5: changing the tool transport from in-process Python calls (`SocEnvironment`) to a shell CLI (`soc-cli` in a Harbor container) was enough to invert the evolved runbooks' advantage, while the human-written skill transferred.

## Prompt and skill optimization

### Agrawal et al. (2025). GEPA: Reflective Prompt Evolution Can Outperform Reinforcement Learning

Lakshya A. Agrawal, et al. arXiv:2507.19457.
KB: not yet harvested. From the KB `main` worktree: `just harvest 2507.19457`.

The optimizer this repository wraps in `agent/gepa_runbook_optimizer.py` (`RunbookGEPAAdapter`, `gepa.optimize(..., candidate_selection_strategy="pareto")`). GEPA mutates text components by having a reflection model read execution traces and propose rewrites, and keeps a Pareto frontier of candidates across validation instances. Everything in the blog post about memorization and bloat is about what an unconstrained reflective proposer does when the traces contain the answers.

### Oved et al. (2026). Optimize Cheap, Deploy Strong: A Recipe for Cost-Efficient GEPA

Tal Oved, Roi Pony, Oshri Naparstek, Udi Barzelay, Lakshya A. Agrawal. `https://gepa-ai.github.io/gepa/blog/2026/09/29/optimize-cheap-deploy-strong/`.
KB: `ingest_rrsi_2026_papers/wiki/papers/oved_2026.md` (also on branch `ingest_gepa_optimize_cheap`).

Run GEPA with a cheap task model and deploy the optimized prompt on a stronger one. Our cost staircase walks the other direction (optimize on `gemini-2.5-flash-lite`, try to deploy on Gemma), and the 2026-10-02 walk did not succeed in that direction at a 20-call budget. The two are not in conflict; they answer different questions, and the asymmetry is worth testing explicitly.

### Tang et al. (2026). WikiSkill: Compiling Agent Experience into Persistent Knowledge for Skill Evolution

Liyan Tang, Cyrus Rashtchian, Chun-Sung Ferng, et al. arXiv:2608.27454.
KB: `ingest_rrsi_2026_papers/wiki/papers/tang_2026.md`.

Cited in `research_plan_secops_skill_evolution.md` as prior work on compiling experience into skills rather than prompts.

### Zhang, Kraska, and Khattab (2025). Recursive Language Models (RLM)

Alex L. Zhang, Tim Kraska, Omar Khattab. MIT CSAIL & Databricks. arXiv:2512.24601. DSPy API: `https://dspy.ai/current/api/modules/RLM/`.

Introduces the Recursive Language Model (`RLM`) inference paradigm in which an LLM explores large contexts and external tools programmatically by writing executable Python in a stateful REPL and invoking recursive `llm_query` sub-calls. Used here in [`agent/rlm_soc_worker.py`](../agent/rlm_soc_worker.py) (`RLMSOCRunbookWorkerAgent`, `GuardedSOCInterpreter`) and evaluated against ReAct across Gen 3 Tier 1 triage and Track 2 6-hop Ursnif threat hunting in [`DSPY_RLM_WORKER_EVALUATION_ANALYSIS.md`](soc_eval_results/DSPY_RLM_WORKER_EVALUATION_ANALYSIS.md).

## Evaluation methodology and hillclimbing discipline

### Bennett (2026). Claude's hillclimb loop, with your traces underneath

Jim Bennett. `https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/claudes-hillclimb-loop-your-traces-underneath-jim-bennett-434gc/`.

### Woodside and Toner (2024). Evaluating Large Language Models

Thomas Woodside, Helen Toner. CSET.
KB: `ingest_rrsi_2026_papers/wiki/papers/woodside_2024.md`.

### Dwork et al. (2015). Generalization in Adaptive Data Analysis and Holdout Reuse

Cynthia Dwork, Vitaly Feldman, Moritz Hardt, Toniann Pitassi, Omer Reingold, Aaron Roth. NeurIPS 28.
KB: not harvested (not an arXiv-first paper in the registry; the arXiv version is 1506.02629).

The theoretical reason a holdout stops being a holdout after enough adaptive queries. RRSI cites it for the evolve-set versus held-out separation. Our validation split is four cases queried repeatedly; the Harbor sandbox is the real held-out.

### Kolesnikov (2026). Your Agent Is Only as Good as the Sandbox You Measured It In

Vlad Kolesnikov. Medium. `https://medium.com/@vladkol_eqwu/your-agent-is-only-as-good-as-the-sandbox-you-measured-it-in-f391b908418c`.
KB: `ingest_kolesnikov_sandbox/wiki/papers/kolesnikov_2026.md`.

Source of the sentence this project set out to test ("Smaller models with good skills matched larger models without them", summarizing SkillsBench) and of the argument that text-only grading is not evidence about execution. The `verbose_lore.md` result is the clearest confirmation we have of the second point.
